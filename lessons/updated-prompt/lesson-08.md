# Lesson 8 — He runs, She plays 🏃‍♀️

> A tricky, exciting step! When we talk about he or she, the verb gets a little
> -s: He runs. She plays.

## 📋 Plan (≈25 min)
1. Warm-up — read sounds + review "I run" (4 min)
2. New idea — add -s for he/she/it (5 min)
3. Pattern + swap — "He/She + verb-s" (6 min)
4. Exercises (8 min)
5. Homework (2 min)

## 🎯 What we will learn
- Grammar: for he/she/it add -s to the verb — runs, plays, eats, likes.
- Compare: I run / He runs; We play / She plays.
- Word: sleep 😴 (спать).

## ✏️ Exercises for Nika
**1. Reading warm-up.** Slide the sounds: runs, plays, eats. Hear the -s.

**2. Flip it.** I say "I run" — you say "He runs." Try: I play → she plays, I eat → he eats, I sleep → she sleeps.

**3. Pattern box — read it out loud.**
> I run.   He runs.   She plays.   The dog eats.   She sleeps.

**4. Swap game.** Keep "She ___" with the -s verb and swap: runs, plays, eats, sleeps. Four sentences.

**5. Say it about your real life.** Say two true sentences about someone you know: "He ___." / "She ___." (My mum cooks. My friend runs.)

> 🇷🇺 This -s is pure English — Russian changes verb endings by person too, but
> differently. The rule is simple: only he/she/it get the extra -s. I/you/we/they
> do not.

## 🏠 Homework (5 min/day)
- Watch someone and report it: "He ___. She ___." with the -s verb.
- Say the pair aloud: "I play — she plays."
