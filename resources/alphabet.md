# 🔤 English Alphabet — sounds & pronunciation (for Nika)

A reference chart for the parent and Nika. For each letter you get:
**the letter · its name · the sound(s) it makes · an example word + picture · the Russian
sound to copy**.

> **Important:** unlike Russian, one English letter often makes **more than one sound**.
> The most common cases are shown. The Russian spellings (in «…») are *approximate* — they
> get you close; listen to a native clip for the real sound where you can.

## 🇷🇺 Watch out — letters that LOOK Russian but sound different

These Latin letters resemble Cyrillic ones and confuse Russian readers the most:

| Latin letter | Russian reader expects… | But it really says |
|---|---|---|
| **a** | «а» | /æ/ — short, bright, as in **cat** |
| **p** | «р» (r) | /p/ — as in **pen** |
| **n** | «п» (p) | /n/ — as in **nose** |
| **c** | «с» (s) | usually /k/ — as in **cat** |
| **e** | «е» (ye) | /e/ — as in **egg** |
| **h** | «н» (n) | /h/ — a soft breath, as in **hat** |
| **y** | «у» (oo) | /j/ — as in **yes** |
| **x** | «х» (kh) | /ks/ — as in **box** |

## 💡 How to use this chart
- **Learning a sound:** point to the letter, say the sound (not the name), then the example
  word while looking at the picture.
- **Two sounds:** teach the **first** (short) sound first — it's the one used in most simple
  words. The second comes later (long vowels, "magic e", etc.).
- **Letter names** («эй, би, си…») are for spelling out loud and the alphabet song — but for
  *reading*, the **sounds** matter most.
- Sing the ABC song for the names, and use this chart for the sounds. Both together! 🎵
