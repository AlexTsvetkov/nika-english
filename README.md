# 🦊 Nika's English Adventure

A playful English course for an 8-year-old Russian-speaking child. **Nika already knows the
alphabet** — the real goal now is **reading**: turning letters into *sounds* and *blending*
those sounds into words. That blending skill is the whole focus of this first stage, built
on phonics methods used in schools around the world (UK synthetic phonics / Jolly Phonics,
Montessori multisensory learning, and Total Physical Response).

Lessons are **short and packed with exercises** — Nika learns to read by *doing*, not
listening. Writing comes along for the ride.

A friendly guide travels with us through the whole course: **Lumi the Fox** 🦊.
Lumi doesn't know English yet either — Nika teaches Lumi everything she learns. (Teaching
someone else is one of the strongest ways to learn. Lumi can be a toy, a drawing, or just
pretend.)

## 🗓️ The rhythm

- **6 months, 2 lessons per week** (≈ 48 lessons total).
- Each lesson is **25–30 minutes**. If Nika is tired, stop early — a happy 15 minutes
  beats a frustrated 30. Short and joyful always wins.
- Best time: when she's rested, not right before bed.
- Same place, same little ritual each time (e.g. start with the "Hello" song). Children
  love predictability.

## 🧩 How every lesson is built (always these 4 parts)

Each lesson file is short and has the same four sections:

1. **📋 Plan** — the quick run-sheet with timings (what happens, in order).
2. **🎯 What we will learn** — the goal in one glance (which sounds, which reading skill).
3. **✏️ Exercises for Nika** — the heart of the lesson: hands-on reading games and drills.
   This is where most of the 25 minutes goes. Reading is a skill — she builds it by doing.
4. **🏠 Homework** — a tiny 5-minutes-a-day task to repeat before the next lesson.

No preparation needed — open the file and follow it top to bottom.

## 🅰️ The golden rules of phonics (please read once)

1. **Teach the SOUND, not the letter name.** Say the letter *s* as a soft hiss
   `/sss/`, **not** "ess". Say *m* as `/mmm/`, not "em". This is the single most important
   rule — it's what lets a child *read*. (Letter names come later.)
2. **Keep sounds "pure" and short.** Say `/t/`, not "tuh"; `/p/`, not "puh". A tiny extra
   "uh" makes blending `c-a-t` into "cat" much harder. Clip it short.
3. **Blending is the magic trick.** Reading = saying sounds fast and sliding them
   together: `c … a … t → ccaat → cat`. We practise this a LOT.
4. **Review every single lesson.** Old sounds come back as warm-ups forever. Repetition
   is not boring to a child — it's comforting and it's how memory is built.
5. **Praise effort, not just success.** "You tried a hard one!" matters more than "correct".

## 🇷🇺 Special notes for a Russian-speaking family

Russian and English share the Latin/Cyrillic look-alikes, which is the #1 source of
confusion. Watch for these **"false friends"** (letters that look familiar but sound
different in English):

| Looks like Russian… | …but in English says |
|---|---|
| `a` (а) | `/æ/` — a short, bright "a" as in **cat** (not Russian "а") |
| `p` (р) | `/p/` as in **pen** (Russian р = "r"!) |
| `n` (п) | `/n/` as in **nose** (Russian п = "p"!) |
| `c` (с) | usually `/k/` as in **cat** (Russian с = "s") |
| `e` (е) | `/e/` as in **egg** (not Russian "ye") |
| `h` (н) | `/h/` — a soft breath, as in **hat** (Russian н = "n"!) |
| `y` (у) | `/y/` as in **yes** (Russian у = "oo") |

Each lesson flags these the moment they appear. 🇷🇺 marks a tip written for **you, the
parent** — including how to make English sounds Russian doesn't have.

**Sounds that don't exist in Russian** (we go slowly on these):
- `/æ/` (cat) — brighter and more open than Russian "э".
- `/h/` (hat) — a gentle breath, NOT the harsh Russian "х".
- `/r/` (red) — soft, the tongue does **not** roll/vibrate like Russian "р".
- short `/ɪ/` (sit) vs long `/iː/` (seat) — a length difference that changes the word.
- later: `/θ/ /ð/` (th), `/w/` (we), `/ŋ/` (sing).

You don't need perfect pronunciation. Learning alongside her is wonderful — do the actions
and songs together. Your enthusiasm teaches more than your accent.

## ✅ When Nika is ready for the next sound

Move on when she can, **without help**:
- say the sound when she sees the letter, and
- find/write the letter when she hears the sound, and
- blend a simple word with it (e.g. read "sat").

If not yet — repeat the game another day. There is no race. 🐢
